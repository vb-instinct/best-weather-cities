Climate atlas · first edition · 13 October 2026

Good weather,
city by city.

The best climate is the one that fits you. Compare cities within a country, find a useful season, and see the numbers behind the choice.

90 city/station candidates. 23 countries. One consistent climate period: 1991-2020. No "perfect weather" promises.

A mild long-season starting point

San Diego, coastal Los Angeles, Funchal, Agadir and Essaouira are worth opening first. Mild averages do not rule out heat, wind, fog or heavy rain.

Temperature and rain are different

Pereira and Coffs Harbour can have mild temperature averages in every month and still be wet. Switch the rule to see what changes.

A season can beat a yearly winner

Mediterranean cities often look strongest in spring or autumn. Choose your month before choosing your city.

A shortlist, not a global census. Ranks compare the included candidates, not every city in the country. "Fit months" counts monthly averages that pass a preference rule. It does not count comfortable days or rank safety, healthcare, visas or living costs.

Find your kind of weather

Teal months pass your rule. Neutral months do not necessarily have bad weather. A selected month sorts each country's cities by fit in that month first, then year-wide fit.

Read the averages, not the hype.

A high of 25 C means the average of daily maximum temperatures in that month over 1991-2020. Some days will be much hotter. The low is the average daily minimum, not a guaranteed overnight temperature.

Rainfall in millimetres measures volume, not hours of ruined plans. Rain days (at least 1 mm) add context. Humidity, sunshine, fog, wind, UV and extreme-event risk are not scored here. Their absence is not evidence that they are harmless.

What makes a month a fit?

  • Mild + drier: average high 18-28 C, low 8-20 C, precipitation at most 100 mm.
  • Mild temperature: the same high and low limits, with no rain limit.
  • Warmer: average high 22-32 C, low 12-24 C, precipitation at most 150 mm.

These are editorial preferences, not a scientific comfort index or health limits. Boundaries are inclusive: a tenth of a degree can change a count. Ties use temperature-fit months, then lower annual rain, then city name. Use monthly values, not tiny rank differences.

For a trip

  1. Choose a country and month. Open the top few city records.
  2. Read the high, low, rain and rain days, then check the station caveat.
  3. Check a live forecast, official warnings, UV, air quality and seasonal hazards before booking.

For a move

Look at your least comfortable months, not just the annual average. Test the actual neighbourhood in the difficult season. Check humidity, housing cooling/heating, healthcare, flood/fire/storm exposure, water supply, visa rules, safety and living costs separately. This atlas has not verified those factors.

Where this is incomplete

Only cities with complete monthly high, low and rain series are ranked. Naples, Catania, Guanajuato, Caxias do Sul and Musanze had incomplete core data. Guatemala station/value anomalies remain unresolved and were withheld. Ecuador and Namibia appear in the inspiration article but are not ranked here. A missing city is not a negative verdict.

Stations are local proxies. Gran Canaria airport is not Las Palmas city, San Francisco airport is not its ocean-facing neighbourhoods, and coastal Fiumicino is not central Rome. Suspect source coordinates were not used to invent a map. See the methodology for the audit trail.

Climate changes; normals lag.

All values use 1991-2020, the latest completed standard 30-year period used here. Retrieval in 2026 does not make them 2026 observations. Recent warmth and future risk can differ. This is climate context, not a forecast.
